Who is struggling, where, and what to do about it

K12Plus Monitor flags a learner who has not been active for a week, who is practising below 40%, who scored low on the start of year test, or who keeps getting the same topic wrong. A teacher sees those learners by name, with the reason beside each one.

Regional offices and the Ministry see the same picture one level up: schools and regions ranked by the share of learners who need support, and the topics that hold them back.

One button, Suggest what to do, asks the K12Plus agent for practical next steps: who to support first, which topic to reteach and how, and when to involve parents or the region. The agent receives figures only. Names stay on the teacher's screen.

Funders see their money at work

Every funder receives its own sign-in to K12Plus Monitor. Funders follow the pilot's results for the whole country in numbers, with no learner names, and open a Funds page for each grant.

  • The grant, what has been spent, what remains and the cost for each learner taking part
  • Spending against time, so it is clear early on if money would run out before the grant ends
  • Every budget line and every expense, with date and reference
  • Monthly running costs calculated automatically, alongside expenses entered by K-Twelve Educational Technologies

What learners see on their side

The figures in K12Plus Monitor come from learning that learners enjoy. Two recent additions help them keep going.

💡

Make me understand this, in my life

One button, or a typed or spoken request, asks the agent to explain an equation through the learner's own world: a taxi fare, a mahangu harvest, a cell phone bundle. It ends with a small question to try, so understanding is checked straight away.

🎮

Game Center

Learners earn coins in tests, quizzes, tasks and practice. New games cost coins, and when a learner does not have enough, the Game Center shows exactly which quiz, test or task will earn them. Practice games in the Game Arcade stay free.
